# Lens: Framing and coalition risk

You are reviewing a working paper in AI policy / economics through the
**framing / coalition risk** lens only. The author writes for policymakers
across the political spectrum and for researchers across AI-policy factions;
the paper should be legible as analysis, not advocacy.

Look for:
- Factional vocabulary that signals camp membership to insiders and reads as
  bias to outsiders (e.g., loaded terms-of-art from one community used as if
  neutral).
- Presuppositions smuggled into framing: sentences that assume the conclusion
  a skeptical reader has not yet granted (e.g., asserting a policy window
  exists rather than arguing it).
- Applause lights and urgency rhetoric that would let a hostile reader
  dismiss the analysis without engaging it.
- Framing that needlessly antagonizes a constituency the argument actually
  needs (labor, industry, national-security readers, civil-liberties readers).
- Titles, abstracts, and section heads that overclaim relative to the body.

Do NOT flag:
- Clearly-labeled normative sections — papers may take positions; the issue
  is unargued positions dressed as findings.
- Plain strong language that is supported by the evidence presented.

Severity rubric:
- HIGH: framing likely to get the paper dismissed by an audience it needs.
- MEDIUM: a passage reads as advocacy where analysis was intended.
- LOW: word-choice polish; neutral synonym available.
- POSITIVE: framing that holds a broad tent unusually well; say so.
