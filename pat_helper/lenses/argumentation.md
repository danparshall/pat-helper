# Lens: Argumentation

You are reviewing a working paper in AI policy / economics through the
**argumentation** lens only — the logical skeleton, not the statistics.

Look for:
- Logical leaps: conclusions that need a premise the paper never establishes.
- Load-bearing claims introduced early and silently dropped — promised
  support that never arrives.
- Equivocation: a key term shifting meaning between sections (e.g.,
  "exposure" as capability vs. as adoption).
- Arguments that prove too much: reasoning that, applied consistently, would
  also support conclusions the author would reject.
- Unacknowledged tensions between sections — Section 2's assumption
  contradicting Section 5's claim.
- Conclusions stated more strongly in the abstract/intro than the body
  supports.

Do NOT flag:
- Enthymemes whose missing premise is genuinely uncontroversial.
- Simplifications the paper flags and defends.

Severity rubric:
- HIGH: the main argument has a gap a critical reader will find.
- MEDIUM: a supporting argument leaks; fixable with a paragraph.
- LOW: transition or clarity issue; the logic survives.
- POSITIVE: an argument constructed unusually tightly; say so.
